Programs

Learning through play, one stage at a time.

How Children Learn Here

Play with a purpose.

Every activity has a goal, even when it looks like fun. Especially then.

Each teacher designs the class curriculum around the children in the room: their stage of development, their interests, their needs and their strengths. New ideas and skills come in step by step, and teachers turn everyday moments into lessons.

While children play, teachers watch how each one is growing and what they understand, then stretch the learning a little further.

What we want children to become

  • Critical thinkers
  • Problem solvers
  • Decision makers
  • Passionate about learning
  • Able to get along with others
  • Ready for kindergarten

What lands in your inbox

Every week, your child’s class emails its curriculum plan and snack menu, so you know what is coming and can talk about it at home.

Toddlers · Age 2

Big discoveries for two-year-olds.

Teacher: Ms. Kristen Costanzo

Toddlers learn through play and hands-on experience: shapes and colors, sensory activities, time outdoors and playful experiments.

Teachers talk children through the day as it happens, a simple habit that builds vocabulary fast.

Most of all, toddlers need to feel secure. Responsive care and close, steady relationships with their teachers give them the confidence to explore.

Toddler enrichment

  • Music & Movement with Ms. Julie: clapping, marching, dancing and jumping to a rhythm.
  • STEM: sink-or-float experiments, building with blocks and spotting simple patterns.
  • Toilet training: part of the daily curriculum. How toilet training works

Preschool · Ages 3–4

Room to choose, try and imagine.

Teacher: Ms. Jill Anderson

Our preschoolers learn through an emergent, play-based curriculum. They make their own choices about play, take small risks, solve problems and use their imagination in open-ended activities.

Teachers set up hands-on experiences and guide play toward real learning, through purposeful play and meaningful conversation.

Where children grow

  • Self-direction
  • Independence
  • Managing feelings
  • Critical thinking
  • Problem solving
  • Thinking skills
  • Language
  • Physical skills

Pre-Kindergarten

The year that gets them kindergarten-ready.

Teacher: Mrs. Norma Guerrero

Pre-kindergarten is structured, fun and focused on the academic and social-emotional skills children need for kindergarten. Experienced teachers guide every step.

Children work in small groups all year to master the skills in our pre-kindergarten assessment, which follows goals comparable to transitional kindergarten (TK):

  • Thinking skills: early math, sorting, patterns and other STEM concepts
  • Early literacy: phonological awareness, hearing the sounds inside words
  • Language
  • Fine and gross motor skills
  • Social-emotional growth
  • Work habits
  • Classroom routines

Thinking ahead? St. Matthias is a feeder school for St. Charles School (K–8) in San Carlos.

After preschool

Toilet Training

Potty training, handled together.

Part of the toddler curriculum, planned with you.

We treat toilet training as an important early learning skill. Standing, sitting, balancing and shifting weight on a potty chair or toilet are real skills, and they are part of the toddler class every day.

Parents and teacher work as partners:

  1. Watch for readiness. You and your child’s teacher decide together when your toddler is ready to start.
  2. Make one plan. You agree on a consistent plan for home and school, so your child gets the same support everywhere.
  3. Build confidence. A shared plan lowers stress for everyone and helps your toddler succeed.

Questions about toilet training? Call the preschool at (650) 367-1320.

Outdoor Classroom

Outside is part of the lesson plan.

Shade trees, open space and room to roam.

Our playground is enormous and park-like, with tall shade trees, a lower yard, climbing structures and paths for bikes and scooters.

Teachers plan outdoor time as carefully as indoor time. Outside, children build strength, endurance, flexibility and balance, explore freely, and learn to love the natural world.

On the playground you might see

  • Bike Day, complete with a drive-through bike wash
  • Chalk drawings across the big wall
  • Balance walks along a line of blocks
  • Digging and planting in the garden
